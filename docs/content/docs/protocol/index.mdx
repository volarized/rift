The protocol is three documents, and the split is not filing: it is the shape of the system.

`core.json` is the model — what Rift reads and what it can say about it. Both of the other two are
defined in terms of it, and neither is defined in terms of the other. `mcp.json` is the surface an agent
calls. `adapter.json` is the wire a language's compiler answers on.

If you are reading this for the first time, read them in that order. The model page is a chain: each idea
exists because the one before it left a question open, and once you have that chain, the other two pages
are mostly a matter of looking things up.

## Why the schemas are the source

The three JSON Schema documents are normative, and these pages render them directly. There is no
generated file in between and no step that could be skipped: change a schema and the page changes with
it, or the build fails.

That matters more than it sounds. A protocol reference that drifts from the protocol is worse than no
reference, because it is confidently wrong. The only prose written by hand here is the part the schema
genuinely cannot state about itself — why a concept exists, and what it looks like in practice.
